## 6. Competition Structure & Tracks

- **Two tracks total.** Everyone starts in **Track 1: Lifestyle & Personal Productivity**.
- Track 1 has **two problem statements** — each team picks **one** (not both):
  1. **Stress & Workload Manager**
  2. **Travel Planner**
- After the Prototype Judging Phase, the **top 10 teams** from Track 1 become finalists and proceed directly to build their full app.
- Teams **not** selected from Track 1 have the *option* (not mandatory) to move into **Track 2: Industry** — a separate, sponsor-backed, **low-code/no-code** track with its own problem statement(s), revealed **21 September** (after Track 1's prototype phase ends). Less build-heavy than Track 1.
  - You cannot choose both problem statements, and a team cannot enter both tracks at once by choice.
  - If you become a Track 1 finalist, you must continue in Track 1 — you cannot also join Track 2 unless you voluntarily give up your Track 1 place.
- Track 2 also selects **10 finalists**.
- **Total: 20 finalists** across both tracks, **3 winners per track = 6 winners overall.** (Not necessarily split evenly between the two problem statements within Track 1 — selection is based on the same rubric regardless of which problem statement a team picked.)

### Problem Statement 1 — Stress & Workload Manager
> Build an app that gives students a clear picture of their load across different areas (mental, time, physical, social, errands) and actually helps them do something about it before burnout hits. It shouldn't just track and report — it should help students rebalance what they're carrying and push them toward recovery (e.g. rest, getting out of the house). Must be usable, accessible, and something students would actually keep open on their phone.

Example directions mentioned (not requirements): a workload visualizer, a stress tracker over time, a load balancer that reprioritizes tasks, recovery "nudges" (e.g. suggesting sleep/a hangout when overloaded).

### Problem Statement 2 — Travel Planner
> Build something that helps people plan a trip from start to finish — budgeting, itinerary building, syncing group preferences, and adjusting when plans change. Should make planning faster/less stressful and work whether solo or in a group.

Example directions mentioned: AI-generated itineraries from budget/interests, group tools to merge preferences and split costs, on-the-fly replanning (e.g. a delayed flight).

**Prototype expectations (important, repeated heavily in Q&A):**
- The prototype phase is about your **idea and UI only** — no working logic/back-end required, and no bonus marks for having one.
- Any format is fine: Figma, Canva, a paper wireframe, or even a static HTML page — all acceptable, as long as it clearly communicates the idea.
- Mobile app, web app, or browser-based — any platform is accepted (judges may ask you to justify the platform choice, e.g. "why web and not mobile").
- You do **not** need to submit source code at the prototype stage — just show the UI (e.g. screenshots).

## 7. Judging Rubrics (100% total)

| Category | Weight | Sub-criteria (with sub-weights) |
|---|---|---|
| **Ideation** | 25% | Visual diagrams/mindmaps (8%) — mindmap, problem tree, or user flow showing how you ideated; Iteration & idea evolution (7%) — documented changes and *dropped* directions ("dead ends are worth points — document them"); Mentor consultation & feedback integration (7%); Breadth of exploration (3%) |
| **Impact** | 20% | Understanding the problem context (5%); Target group alignment (5%); Effectiveness of the solution (7%); Reach & scalability (3%) |
| **Creativity & Novelty** | 15% | Originality (7%); Novel features/twists (5%); Differentiation from existing solutions (3%) |
| **Feasibility** | 15% | Technical viability/tech stack (6%); Planning & scope realism (5%); Resource & time awareness (4%) |
| **Presentation** | 15% | Clarity of explanation (5%); Structure & flow (4%); Delivery & confidence (4%); Engagement & persuasiveness (2%) |
| **Design** | 10% | Visual consistency (4%); Usability/UX (4%); Mockup completeness (2%) |

**Key takeaway stated explicitly:** *Ideation + Impact = 45% of your score* — before design/polish is even considered. "A beautiful prototype with no documented process will lose to a paper wireframe with a well-documented journey."

## 8. Mentorship Guide

- **Period:** 31 Aug – 13 Sep 2026 (ends same day as the submission deadline).
- Fully **virtual, on Discord**.
- Each session: **max 25 minutes**.
- Mentors are **not assigned** to teams — they post available slots in a shared Excel sheet; booking is **first-come-first-served**, so book early.
- Mentors list their domain (AI, web, mobile, UX, pitching, etc.) — pick based on what you actually need.
- Mentors will **not** design your solution or write code for you — they ask questions, challenge assumptions, and point out best practices. The idea stays yours.
- **At least one mentorship session is effectively mandatory**: skipping it costs you the "mentor feedback integration" points (7% of the Ideation score).
- You *can* book multiple different mentors, but **only one booking per day** (there are 200+ teams and only 18 mentors, so slots are limited).
- Only one team representative needs to attend a session, though bringing more members is encouraged.
- Etiquette: be punctual, don't edit other teams' slots, and if you can't make a slot, remove your team's name at least 2 hours in advance so another team can use it.

## 9. Submission Guidelines

- **Deadline: 13 September 2026, 11:59 PM** — submit via a **Google Form** (team leader submits on behalf of the team; late submissions are not accepted — aim to submit 15 minutes early).
- **Deliverables**, all linked from your **public GitHub repo's README.md**:
  - Project overview
  - Ideation/process assets (documentation of your idea evolution — see below)
  - Design/prototype links (Figma, Canva, static HTML, etc. — must be set to public/viewable)
  - A **3–5 minute video** on YouTube, **set to unlisted**, named with your team name
- The GitHub repo itself doesn't need to contain your actual code (unless you happen to have built something) — the README is essentially a hub of links; make sure the repo is public and everything linked is viewable.
- **"Documenting your process"** (repeatedly clarified in Q&A) means: screenshots/records of your mindmaps, the ideas you explored and *discarded* (and why), and what you did with mentor feedback — not a report of finished features. No fixed template is provided.
- Video content guidance: mostly explain your solution/app (what it does, why); keep detailed process documentation in the README rather than the video. A suggested split mentioned in Q&A: ~2 min on ideation, ~2 min on the UI/demo, ~1 min on tech stack — the goal is to make judges curious.
