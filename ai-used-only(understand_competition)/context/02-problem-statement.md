# Problem Statement — Stress & Workload Manager

*Track 1: Lifestyle & Personal Productivity*

## The Brief (verbatim from the organizers)

> University students are always juggling everything at once: assignments, part-time jobs, social commitments, errands, and just staying physically and mentally okay. Burnout is rarely one big thing, though — it's everything piling up together without anyone noticing until it's too late.
>
> Build an app that gives students a clear picture of their load across different areas (mental, time, physical, social, errands) and actually helps them do something about it before burnout hits.
>
> **It shouldn't just track and report.** It should help students rebalance what they're carrying and push them toward recovery — like rest or getting out of the house. Make sure it's usable, accessible, and something students would actually keep open on their phone.

## Example Directions (organizer-suggested, not requirements — any approach is valid as long as it satisfies the brief)

- A **workload visualizer** across life domains
- A **stress tracker** over time
- A **load balancer** that groups tasks and pushes lower-priority ones back
- **Recovery nudges** — e.g. suggesting sleep, downtime, or a hangout when someone is clearly overloaded

## What "solving this well" looks like, per the rubric (see `03-judging-rubric.md` for exact wording)

- Name a **specific** target user, not "all students" (Target Group Alignment, 5%)
- Show a clear **before/after**: what life looks like without the app vs. with it (Effectiveness, 7%)
- Go beyond tracking — the app needs a genuine mechanism that pushes the user toward *doing something* about their load, not just visualizing it
- A path to reach beyond the first pilot group counts as a (small) bonus (Reach & Scalability, 3%)

## What the Prototype Phase Actually Requires (repeatedly clarified by organizers)

- **UI/UX only.** No working logic, no back-end, no working buttons required. This is explicitly true even if the team has the skills to build more — no extra credit for a working prototype at this stage, and no penalty either way.
- Any format works: Figma, Canva, a static HTML page, or hand-drawn paper wireframes/screenshots — clarity of the idea is what's judged, not the tool.
- Any platform is acceptable — mobile, web, or browser-based — but be ready to justify the platform choice if asked (e.g. "why web and not a mobile app for something meant to stay open on a phone?").
- Full working code/back-end only gets built later, *if and only if* selected as one of the 10 Track 1 finalists (Building Phase, 21 Sep – 11 Oct).

## Non-Requirements to Avoid Wasted Effort

- Don't build a working database/auth/backend for the prototype submission — it won't be scored on that, and time is better spent on ideation documentation and UI clarity.
- Don't try to cover both problem statements — only "Stress & Workload Manager" was chosen; a submission must pick one.
